01 // VerificationIs it real?

A face, a voice or a document can now be generated cheaply. Treat media as a claim to be tested, not proof. These checks work whether the item turns out to be real or fake.

Photo or video

Phone or video call

Document or ID

In court"It's a deepfake" is a claim that needs evidence, in either direction. In R. v. Medow, 2025 ONCJ 661, a bare allegation that body-cam video was faked was rejected.

02 // PreventionWhat to tell the public

Short lines that work at a counter, on a call or in a media release.

Assume it's fake until verified

If a video shows a public figure promoting an investment, assume it is fake. The Prime Minister does not sell crypto platforms.

Set a family code word

A short recording is enough to clone a voice. A code word defeats the "grandchild in trouble" call.

Never pay in crypto or gift cards

No bank, police service or government agency asks for payment this way.

Fake gains are the hook

A dashboard showing your money growing is the scam. Try a small withdrawal before investing more.

04 // Legal processAI in the cloud

If the suspect used ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or similar, the chats sit on the provider's servers. Most providers are US companies. Move early.

  1. Preserve first

    Preservation demand under s. 487.012 Cr.C., and a voluntary preservation request to the provider. OpenAI's published policy preserves data for up to 90 days on request. Deleted chats can be gone from some providers within 30 days.

  2. Then compel

    Production order (s. 487.014) for Canadian-held data. For US-held content, get advice early on the cross-border route (MLAT or provider policy). Check each provider's law-enforcement guidelines.

  3. Ask for the right things

    Subscriber and payment details, IP and login history, chat content, uploaded files, generated images, and any safety flags or account enforcement.

  4. Imminent harm

    Providers run emergency disclosure processes for threats to life. A provider referral is possible but never guaranteed; no Canadian law requires AI companies to report threats.

Reference: OpenAI Government User Data Request Policy (PDF). Not legal advice; confirm with your legal services or Crown.

2026

07 // TermsGlossary

Large language model (LLM)
AI that writes text by predicting the next word. Fluent is not the same as true.
Hallucination
A confident, invented answer: fake quotes, case law or facts.
Agent
AI that takes actions (browses, clicks, runs code, sends email) toward a goal, not just answers.
Local model
A model downloaded and run on the user's own computer. No provider sees the prompts; evidence stays on the device.
Open-weight model
A model whose files are published for anyone to download (Llama, Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek).
Uncensored
An open model retrained so it no longer refuses harmful requests.
Abliterated
An open model with its "refusal direction" removed directly from the weights. Cheap, fast, and thousands are on Hugging Face.
Jailbreak
A prompt that tricks a mainstream AI into ignoring its safety rules, e.g. "it's just a test" or "it's fiction".
GGUF
A common file format for local language models (.gguf), used by LM Studio, Ollama and llama.cpp.
Safetensors
A file format for model weights (.safetensors), common for image models and LoRAs.
LoRA
A small add-on file that teaches an image model one face, style or object. A LoRA named after a real person is significant.
Deepfake
Media where a real person's face or voice is generated or swapped in.
Voice clone
A synthetic copy of a real voice made from a short recording.
Content Credentials (C2PA)
A signed record attached to a file describing how it was made or edited. Its absence proves nothing.
SynthID
Google's invisible watermark in AI images, audio and video from its own tools.
Prompt injection
Hidden instructions in a web page or document that hijack an AI agent reading it.
Hugging Face
The largest public library of AI models. Legitimate, and also where most uncensored models are published.

No terms match. Try a shorter word.
